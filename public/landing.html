01

The paper problem.

Why a digital tool, when paper already worked?
scattered.

Lists live everywhere.

Work sheet on the desk. Family list on the fridge. SWE goals in a notebook. None of them know about each other.

cluttered.

Done items pile up.

By Wednesday the sheet is half-ticked, mostly noise. Re-copying the survivors onto a fresh page is the entire ritual.

out of sync.

Today & Week drift.

Today’s list says “reply to Andre.” The category sheet still has it unchecked. Now both are wrong.

02

A single screen, shaped like paper.

Same workflow. None of the friction.

The whole app is one screen, split 70/30. The left is your board: a wall of category cards, each with a checklist. The right is the Today / Week panel — the same items, re-grouped by horizon. Tick a task anywhere and it’s done everywhere. At midnight, finished items quietly fall away on their own.

“Brainstorm freely.
Tick what’s done.
Carry the rest.”

Feature 04

Lists that clean themselves.

At local midnight, finished items disappear from Today. At Monday 00:00, finished items disappear from Week. Anything still un-ticked carries over — nothing important is silently dropped. The underlying task on the card is left alone.

Feature 06

Your data, on your machine.

Garap lives in your browser’s IndexedDB. No server, no sync, no account. For backup or moving between machines, export everything to a JSON file and import it back. The file is the format. It’s yours.

export.json
A single, readable file. Diffable. Versionable. Yours.
{ "boards": [ { "id": "b1", "name": "Work — Q2" }, { "id": "b2", "name": "Dad" } ], "cards": [...], "items": [ { "id": "i17", "cardId": "c4", "name": "Review PR #142", "completed": false } ], "todayRefs": [{ "itemId": "i17" }], "weekRefs": [] }
import → replace

On import, the file replaces everything currently in the browser. A confirmation prompt makes the destructive step explicit — no merge, no migration, no hidden state.

✓ disaster-recovery in one click.

Colophon.

Garap is a deliberately small piece of software. It was built to replace a stack of paper sheets and nothing more. There is no account system, no cloud, no analytics, no AI assist. Your boards, cards, and items live in your browser’s IndexedDB — the same place your bookmarks and history live. The JSON export is the only way data leaves the device, and that’s entirely on you.

The aesthetic is on purpose, too. The thing it replaces was a paper notebook, so the screen is shaped like one: cream, ruled, ink. The two markers (red for Today, green for done) are the only colors that carry information. Everything else is restraint.
